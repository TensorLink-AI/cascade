Performance — forecast improvement vs baseline

The baseline, and why it does not move

The baseline is the generator that held the throne at the first round in the public receipt record.

Improvement is chained, not re-measured. The subnet’s eval pool rotates every round (seeded by the block hash) so no generator can distribution-match it — which also means a raw error number from round 1 and one from round 80 were measured on different windows and are not comparable. What is comparable is each dethrone: king and challenger are scored on the same windows, same round, so the ratio between them is a clean paired measurement. Multiplying those ratios across every dethrone gives the cumulative improvement over the launch baseline, with window difficulty cancelling at each step.

improvement = 1 − Π(challenger_error ÷ king_error) over dethrone rounds. The conservative floor replaces each factor with the round’s 95% bootstrap lower bound — the number the dethrone was actually gated on. Both are plotted; the gap between them is the selection bias (a dethrone is granted on a favourable noisy estimate, so the observed chain runs optimistic).
